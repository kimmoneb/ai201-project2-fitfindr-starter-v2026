# Acceptance criteria — FitFindr

Five criteria that say what "working" means for this agent, written in unit 3
**before** any results existed.

An acceptance criterion names a target: a number, a count, a rate, or something
a person could plainly observe. *"The agent handles errors"* is an opinion.
*"When search returns nothing, the agent stops before calling the second tool,
in 5 of 5 tries"* is a criterion.

Under each one, write a sentence or two on **why that target** and not a
stricter one. A reason that says something about your tools, your loop, or the
data earns credit; *"80% seemed reasonable"* does not.

> Missing your own targets next unit costs you nothing. Setting a target so
> easy you can't miss it does.

**Two are written for you. You write three.**

---

## 1. A matching query completes all three tools

Given a query that matches at least one listing, the agent completes all three
tool calls and returns a fit card — in at least 4 of 5 tries.

**Why this target:**
<!-- Why 4 of 5 and not 5 of 5? Something about your search, probably —
     "my search is a plain keyword match and some phrasings will miss" is a
     real answer. -->
I chose 4 out of 5 because the search depends on matching the user's query to listing data, and some query phrasing may not produce a match. Most matching queries should still successfully complete all three tools and return a fit card.
---

## 2. An impossible query stops before the second tool

Given a query that matches no listings, the agent stops before calling
`suggest_outfit` and returns a message naming what to change — 5 of 5 tries.

**Why this target:**
<!-- Why is 5 of 5 reasonable here when criterion 1 isn't? What's different
     about this path? -->
I chose 5 out of 5 because when no listings match, the agent should consistently stop before calling the next tool. It should also give the user a useful message explaining what they can change instead of continuing with missing results.

## 3. The selected item remains consistent between tools

The item selected from `search_listings` must remain the same item stored in `session["selected_item"]` and passed to `suggest_outfit` in 5 out of 5 tries.

**Why this target:**

I chose 5 out of 5 because the selected item is passed through the session state, so it should remain consistent between tools. Any mismatch could result in outfit recommendations for the wrong item.


**Why this target:**
I chose 5 out of 5 because the selected item is passed through the session state, so it should remain the same between tools every time.

## 4. The fit card includes essential listing information

The generated fit card must include a recognizable description of the selected item, its price, and the selling platform in at least 4 out of 5 tries.

**Why this target:**

I chose 4 out of 5 because the fit card is generated by a model, so the wording may vary between runs. However, the card should consistently provide important listing details that help users understand the item and where to purchase it.


---

## 5. An empty wardrobe still produces styling advice

When the user's wardrobe is empty, the agent should generate useful general styling suggestions and complete a fit card in at least 4 out of 5 tries.

**Why this target:**

I chose 4 out of 5 because styling suggestions are generated by a model and may vary between runs. However, users should still receive helpful outfit recommendations even when they have no existing wardrobe items.
